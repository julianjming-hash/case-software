Case · design system

An open forum, laid tile by tile.

Case is a facilitator-led decision theatre: everyone contributes, difference is welcome, and the facilitator decides which authored path the session follows. The system is quiet and neutral where people read and decide, and expressive where they arrive and gather. Its signature is the mosaic: individual tesserae laid into the case's decision paths.

Principles

Ten owner-approved principles · 3 November 2026 (CASE-353). These supersede the earlier six-rule summary on this living page; the dated decision log preserves that history. Numeric tokens and the source mapping above remain authoritative.

Preferred terms and exact meanings: product lexicon. Layout decisions: current surface specification. Access and retention remain governed by their existing contracts.

1 · Use precise vocabulary

Present opens Lobby; Start case reveals the opening slide (CASE-358, 4 November 2026). Labels introduce no new runtime prerequisites. The product lexicon lives in HANDOFF, not in a parallel glossary.

2 · Preserve Case’s visual meaning

Use existing tokens, typography and mosaic grammar: expressive arrival, calm work. Art represents structure, never participants, votes or clinical correctness. Colour always has a textual or symbolic cue; private styling supplements enforced boundaries.

3 · One primary per active task

Keep teaching controls stable. In horizontal actions, cancel/back goes left and accept right. Destructive placement never makes the destructive action the keyboard default.

4 · Reuse anatomy and behaviour; arrange by task

Share cards, controls and modal frames; use whitespace before extra boxes. Follow the current surface specification’s detail reading order rather than inventing a competing layout per page.

5 · Keep useful information

Remove filler without removing orientation, accessible headings or recovery help. Unknown creator says Not recorded; summaries and status reflect evidence, not decoration.

6 · Report confirmed outcomes

Retain failed-form inputs. Reconcile uncertain results before retrying; a timeout never proves that nothing changed. Success/failure notices use accessible non-reflowing toasts; input validation remains associated with its field.

7 · Distinguish modal states

Clean modals dismiss. Dirty forms show a discard overlay with Keep editing focused. Pending/uncertain writes retain safeguards. Escape affects the top layer; restore focus to the initiating control or its replacement.

8 · Explicit accessible disclosure

Use named Show/Hide actions, keyboard operation and expanded state, without chevrons. Retain the established Create Outline branch-chip exception as an internal implementation name; its product destination is Create.

9 · Measure accessibility

Verify existing 44-pixel targets, contrast, keyboard order/focus, reduced motion, 200% enlargement and applicable 400% / 320-CSS-pixel reflow. Keep focused controls unobscured. A title over artwork needs a quiet text layer and a measured fallback for long titles.

10 · Make scope/privacy truthful

Role is self-described attribution and grants no Access. Removing a case grant may leave workspace access. Your recap, Participant record and retained Session record have distinct meanings and existing retention/access rules.

Code legibility

Session and pairing codes use the Crockford alphabet, which has no I, L, O or U. Typing O becomes 0, and I or L becomes 1 (normalizeCode). JetBrains Mono gives 0 a dot and keeps 1, 7, 2 and Z distinct at projector size.

Sign-in code: 6 digits from email. It is a different component with a different label, so it is never confused with a session code.

Corner radius

Radius scales with size, as in Apple's interfaces: small things are nearly square, large surfaces are soft. Nested corners are concentric. Where the browser supports continuous corners (corner-shape: squircle) they are used; otherwise the same radii apply.

  • Concentric rule: inner radius = outer radius − padding, never below --radius-xs. A 20 px panel with 12 px padding holds 8 px controls.
  • No capsules except avatars and switches. Phase and status are small chips (--radius-chip 6 px) led by a tessera.
  • One radius per element type, whatever the screen. Projector chips scale their padding, not their roundness.
  • Covers use --radius. Inside a card or board item they drop to the concentric value.

Mosaic grammar: the braid

A case's art is drawn from its authored graph. The petrol spine is the default route (each decision's recommended path). Every decision is one plaited arch whose rows are its alternative routes, rejoining the spine where the case does. The renderer, not an illustrator, enforces these rules (design/mosaic.mjs).

Do

  • Colour a plait by its decision, rotating coral → ochre → periwinkle along the case.
  • Keep tile size constant within a rendition; let case density choose tile size (finer for bigger cases).
  • Use the same composition for participants and facilitators.

Don't

  • Encode classification, safety, correctness or the recommended answer in colour, row order or shape.
  • Drop or merge a route to tidy a cover; flag the version instead.
  • Overlay translucent ribbons, gradients, gloss, glass or grain, or put text and controls on tesserae.

Structure and identity

Each layer of input drives one visual layer, so edits behave predictably. The MVC specimens come from the real fixture src/fixtures/synthetic-workshop.case.json; the others are synthetic descriptors with real-case shapes.

From an authored case to its art

  1. Publish (Create). describeCase(document) walks the default route and classifies every other path: a strand rejoins a later stop, a reset returns to its checkpoint, an end never returns. The builder then solves the layout (0.1–2.5 s) and adds the chosen attempt.
  2. Verify (Worker). The Worker re-derives the descriptor from the published document. A mismatch refuses the publish. The descriptor is stored with the immutable version.
  3. Render (clients). Clients receive only the descriptor. Covers draw once to a cached canvas; splash art draws as SVG so tiles can move.

The descriptor is numbers only: no ids, labels, classifications, flags or text. Real MVC fixture:

{"v":1,"stops":5,"decisions":[{"stop":1,"strands":[{"rejoin":2},{"rejoin":2}],"resets":1,"ends":0},{"stop":2,"strands":[{"rejoin":3},{"rejoin":3}],"resets":0,"ends":0},{"stop":3,"strands":[{"rejoin":4},{"rejoin":4}],"resets":0,"ends":0}]}

Failure is explicit. The study tries up to 16 deterministic layouts. If no layout shows every branch legibly, the result is flagged unavailable and the cover shows the unavailable state above. A branch is never dropped silently.

Measured capacity (braid with plaits, 10 seeds per row, Node): every case up to 8 decisions × 2 alternatives with resets, and up to 6 × 3, renders on every seed. 7 × 3 and 9 or more decisions are flagged unavailable. The layout search takes 0.1–2.5 s per case (run once, in the builder at publish). A solved layout renders in about 70–160 ms.

Accessibility

  • Contrast is at least 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text and non-text in both themes. The colour tables above calculate it live, and the shipped tests/contrast.test.mjs remains the gate.
  • Targets are at least 44×44 px. Ranking uses named Move up, Move down and Remove buttons; drag is never the only way.
  • Focus is a 3 px --focus ring with 2 px offset. Dialogs and the menu trap focus, close on Escape and return focus to their trigger.
  • Status changes (receipts, connection, normalised code) use role="status". Blocking errors use role="alert".
  • Artwork is decorative (aria-hidden) on splash surfaces. On covers it is "Abstract branching mosaic artwork", with no description of outcomes. Forced-colours mode hides splash art.
  • Reduced motion removes the cover lift and all transitions. Nothing essential animates.
  • Text only: case content and names render as text, never HTML (hard rule 14). The mosaic SVG is generated from numbers alone.
